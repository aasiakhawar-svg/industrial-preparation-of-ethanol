Panel 1: the two reactions

C₆H₁₂O₆(aq) →(yeast enzymes, 25–35 °C, no air) 2C₂H₅OH(aq) + 2CO₂(g)
C₂H₄(g) + H₂O(g) ⇌(H₃PO₄ catalyst, 300 °C, 60–70 atm) C₂H₅OH(g)

The second reaction is reversible, so some ethene is left over. The first makes a by-product, carbon dioxide. That difference matters for atom economy: fermentation gives (2 × 46) ÷ 180 × 100 = 51%, while hydration gives 46 ÷ (28 + 18) × 100 = 100%, because everything ends up as ethanol.

🌡️300 °C
The reaction gives out heat, so a lower temperature would give more ethanol, but too slowly. 300 °C is a compromise.
🗜️60–70 atm
2 gas molecules become 1, so high pressure pushes towards ethanol. More pressure costs more, so it’s a compromise.
⚗️Catalyst
Phosphoric acid on silica speeds up the reaction without changing the yield or being used up.

Panel 2: why those conditions (Route B)

Only about 5% of the ethene reacts each time it passes through the reactor. Cooling the gases turns the ethanol into a liquid, and the unreacted ethene is recycled round again. Little is wasted.

🌽Raw material
Sugar cane, sugar beet, or starch from maize, which enzymes turn into glucose
🦠Yeast
Its enzymes catalyse the reaction. It dies if the mixture is too hot or too alcoholic
🍺Max about 15%
Yeast is poisoned above this, so the product is dilute

Panel 3: what Route A needs

Large steel fermenters are cooled to stay at 25–35 °C and kept free of air. Oxygen would oxidise the ethanol to ethanoic acid. Fractional distillation then separates ethanol (boils at about 78 °C) from water (100 °C). The CO₂ can be collected and sold, and spent yeast can be used as animal feed.

Panel 5: a decision, like an exam question

A country has huge sugar cane farms but no oil. Fermentation fits: the sugar is local and renewable, it needs no crude oil, and it runs at low temperature and pressure. The slower rate and the distillation step are the trade-offs.

A country with oil refineries and a big chemical industry would likely choose hydration: ethene is on hand, it is fast and continuous, and it gives a purer product.

Panel 6: where ethanol ends up

⛽Fuel

🧴Solvent

Crops absorb CO₂ as they grow, so bioethanol can lower net emissions, though farmland used for fuel can’t grow food.

Check yourself: exam-style questions
  1. Name the catalyst and give the conditions for making ethanol from ethene. Phosphoric acid; about 300 °C; 60–70 atm.
  2. Why must air be kept out of a fermenter? Oxygen would oxidise the ethanol to ethanoic acid.
  3. Why is unreacted ethene recycled? Only about 5% reacts per pass; recycling raises the overall yield and saves raw material.
  4. Calculate the atom economy of fermentation. (2 × 46) ÷ 180 × 100 = 51%.
  5. Give two advantages of fermentation over hydration. Renewable raw material; low temperature and pressure; no crude oil needed.
  6. Give two disadvantages of fermentation. Slow batch process; impure, dilute product that needs distilling.
